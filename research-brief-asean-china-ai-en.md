## 1. Bottom Line

- **Where the Foundation fits.** ASEAN–China AI cooperation now spans almost every AI layer, yet nothing in it helps the people and businesses that use AI once training ends. That stage, after training, is where the Foundation can offer something neither side has.
- **Lead with MSMEs.** Open with the CIIS remarks, then offer evidence and measurement before proposing any joint programme. The case rests on AIM ASEAN and the Singapore National Policy Convening.
- **Teachers second, and conditionally.** Take up teacher cooperation only once it is confirmed whether China’s Smart Education covers teachers, and after informing SOM-ED.
- **One precondition above all.** Confirm what Google.org, AVPN and the ADB allow before offering content or cooperation to a third party.
- **Next decision point.** The Manila Summit in November 2026 will decide whether to adopt a 2027 ASEAN–China Year of AI Cooperation and a leaders’ declaration on AI-driven MSME growth.

## 2. What ASEAN and China Have Built

Two years after the October 2024 Joint Statement on a sustainable and inclusive digital ecosystem, cooperation has institutions at almost every AI layer.

| AI layer | What exists | What to note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governance and safety | Ministerial roundtable on AI (since September 2025); AI ethics and safety cooperation initiative for ASEAN (September 2026) | Initiative details not public. ASEAN keeps its own frameworks: the ASEAN Guide on AI Governance and Ethics and ASEAN AI Safe |
| Industrial application | China–ASEAN AI Industry Innovation Centre (Beijing, May 2026); AI Application Cooperation Centre (Nanning); casebook of 31 cases | Weighted towards infrastructure, smart manufacturing and logistics (as reported by Chinese state media) |
| Talent | Ministry of Science and Technology three-year plan (2025–2027); 5,000 AI training opportunities for developing countries; Digital Academy; Smart Education | Counted in numbers trained. ASEAN’s share and Smart Education’s target groups not confirmed |
| Language and data | Lao national language model; ASEAN AI open-source community | Early stage |

**[Assessment]** China offers AI as a package, from infrastructure to standards, through channels spread across ministries and Guangxi. Its talent programmes count participants but say nothing about how people use AI afterwards. In July 2026 the Foreign Ministers’ Meeting noted a proposal for a 2027 Year of AI Cooperation, so both sides may soon look for deliverables. CIIS sits under China’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs: remarks at the roundtable should be prepared on the assumption that they will be relayed to the Chinese government.

## 3. The Gap: What Happens after Training

The evidence points to the same break: people and firms are trained, but do not go on to use AI in their work.

- **Readiness is low.** 72.4 per cent of students and 70.5 per cent of educators have never taken an AI course, and educators’ measured AI literacy falls short of their confidence (AI Ready ASEAN Research, 2026).
- **Strong support does not close the gap.** In Singapore, 23.4 per cent of SMEs had adopted AI in 2025, against 70.4 per cent of other firms (IMDA). The Singapore Convening found that access to tools is not integration, and that support breaks off between awareness, experimentation, implementation and scaling. Micro enterprises are the most constrained.
- **Elsewhere the barriers are more basic.** The Indonesia Convening pointed to gaps in digital and financial literacy.

| Stage | Where support breaks off | ASEAN–China cooperation today | Foundation entry point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | Chatbot use mistaken for readiness | Training and seminars | AI Class ASEAN; AIM ASEAN courses |
| Experimentation | Unclear use cases; cost of failure | Application scenarios and company matchmaking (Nanning) | Low-risk demonstrations; guidance on responsible use |
| Implementation | Little in-house capacity; staff anxiety | Not found in public sources | Local partners; clinics after training (proposed) |
| Scaling | Support ends with the workshop; no evidence of results | Not found in public sources | Common outcome indicators; regional case exchange (recommended) |

**[Assessment]** The gap is not a lack of AI training: Chinese companies and platforms already provide it. It is the absence of ASEAN-led, technology-neutral support after training, and of any measure of whether training changes how people work.

## 4. What the Foundation Should Offer

The Foundation brings three assets: a delivery network (more than 3,000 Master Trainers and local partners), evidence (the AI readiness research and the convening records), and a role its Singapore partner has recommended: a common pathway after training, common outcome indicators and regional case exchange (subject to agreement). The offer follows the three key summaries of the Singapore report: link training to implementation, build AI-bilingual teams, and measure responsible use and business outcomes.

| Priority | Option | Channel | Timing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **CIIS remarks**: the ‘after training’ question, the Singapore lessons, and the case for pathways, a wider view of AI talent and measuring sustained use | CIIS roundtable, Session 2 | October 2026 |
| 2 | **Measurement**: indicators of sustained use and business value that separate reach, business integration and verified outcomes | AIM ASEAN partners; joint research with CIIS | 2027, after the Singapore baseline (Q4 2026) and review (Q1 2027) |
| 3 | **Case evidence**: structured MSME cases, including what did not work, and open materials for the stage after training | ACCMSME; ASEAN–China forums (public cases only) | 2027 |
| 4 | **Joint MSME programme**: linking AIM ASEAN to the Innovation Centre’s regional capacity building | ACCMSME with China’s industry and commerce ministries | 2027, after reporting to ACCMSME and securing follow-up funding |
| 5 | **Low-cost content**: AI Class ASEAN as pre-learning; an AI ethics and safety module for citizens and MSMEs | Chinese training programmes; ADGSOM and WG-AI | First half of 2027 |
| 6 | **Teachers** (conditional): division of roles and common standards, building on AI Ready ASEAN Phase 2 | SOM-ED with China’s Ministry of Education | 2027–2028 |

All options rest on ASEAN ownership, working through member states, openness to other Dialogue Partners and technology neutrality. The Foundation will not provide learner data or state ASEAN positions on AI standards and norms.

**Sequence.** Share experience now (October–November 2026). After the Manila Summit, build the evidence in the first half of 2027. Open joint programmes only after SOM-ED and ACCMSME have decided. If the Year of AI Cooperation is adopted, a package on users’ AI competence could be one of its deliverables.

## 5. Risks to Manage

| Risk | Response |
| --- | --- |
| **Funders.** It is unclear how far the Google.org, AVPN and ADB agreements allow content sharing and third-party cooperation, amid US–China rivalry | Consult the funders before the visit. Frame the offer as sharing experience of an ASEAN public good |
| **Technology neutrality.** AIM ASEAN promotes the ‘use of Google’s suite of AI tools’, and China may make its own models a condition | Describe training in terms of competence, not tools, and leave the choice of tools to member states. Cite the Singapore finding of no single preferred model, and the Foundation’s work with both Google and Huawei |
| **Mandate.** The Foundation does not represent ASEAN, and remarks at CIIS will be relayed to the Chinese government | Speak from the Foundation’s experience. Do not endorse Chinese initiatives such as the Global AI Governance Action Plan or WAICO. Coordinate with the ASEAN Secretariat, SOM-ED and ACCMSME |
| **Proposals versus commitments.** The Singapore follow-up is still a proposal, and the LIP report is internal | Say ‘under consideration’ and give no dates. Use only public or anonymised material, and clear case examples with the LIP |
| **Member-state sensitivities.** Members differ in how far they cooperate with China on AI; Lao PDR and Indonesia were reported among WAICO’s signatories | Make participation opt-in, and use agreed ASEAN language |
